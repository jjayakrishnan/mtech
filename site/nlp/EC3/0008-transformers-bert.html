RNNs, Attention & BERT

From sequential loops → memory gates → spotlight attention → the Transformer revolution → contextual BERT embeddings

1 · The Encoder-Decoder Architecture

The core problem
Translating word-for-word fails. "Je t'aime" → "I you love" (wrong). You need to read the entire source, build a mental picture, then produce the target. That is exactly what an Encoder-Decoder does.

How it works

The Encoder reads the input and compresses it into a fixed-size Context Vector (like zipping a document into a single binary blob). The Decoder unpacks that blob to produce the output.

Input sequence ──► [ ENCODER ] ──c──► [ DECODER ] ──► Output sequence "Le chat est noir" compresses context generates "The cat is black" into vector vector c
The Bottleneck Problem
The encoder must fit all meaning — whether 5 words or 500 — into one fixed-size vector. Long sentences lose details. This is what Attention was invented to solve.

2 · Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs)

Everyday picture — the bucket of water
The RNN hidden state \(h_t\) is a bucket of water. You start with clear water (\(h_0 = 0\)). Each new word is a drop of coloured dye. When "I" arrives (red dye), the bucket turns red. When "don't" arrives (blue dye), it mixes purple. The final colour captures the whole sentence so far.

The formula

🧠 Must memorise — RNN state update
$$h_t = \tanh(W_h \, h_{t-1} + W_x \, x_t + b)$$

\(h_t\) = new hidden state  ·  \(W_h\) = weight on old memory  ·  \(W_x\) = weight on new word  ·  \(\tanh\) squashes to \([-1, 1]\) to prevent explosion.

Worked example — "I don't like NLP"

Word vectors (2D for illustration):

"I"=[1,0] "don't"=[0,−1] "like"=[1,1] "NLP"=[−1,1]

Weights: \(W_h = \begin{bmatrix}0.5&0\\0&0.5\end{bmatrix}\), \(W_x = I_2\), \(b=0\), \(h_0=[0,0]^T\)

Step 1 — "I": \(h_1 = \tanh(W_h [0,0]^T + [1,0]^T) = \tanh([1,0]^T) \approx [0.76, 0]^T\)

Step 2 — "don't": \(h_2 = \tanh(0.5 \times [0.76,0]^T + [0,-1]^T) = \tanh([0.38,-1]^T) \approx [0.36,-0.76]^T\)

The positive first dimension still holds "I" context; the negative second captures negation from "don't". The state blends all words seen so far.

The Vanishing Gradient — Telephone Game
Imagine whispering a message through 20 people. By the end, the original message is garbled. In an RNN, gradients shrink as they flow backwards through many time steps. Long-distance dependencies (like "cats … are") get forgotten.

3 · Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM)

Everyday picture — the conveyor belt
The RNN is a Telephone Game (message degrades). The LSTM is a conveyor belt with a highway lane (\(C_t\), the Cell State). Workers (Gates) stand alongside. One removes a package (Forget Gate). Another adds one (Input Gate). If workers do nothing, the package travels untouched — gradients flow back undistorted.

The four gates

🧠 Must memorise — LSTM equations
$$f_t = \sigma(W_f \cdot [h_{t-1}, x_t] + b_f) \quad \text{(Forget Gate — what to erase from } C_{t-1})$$ $$i_t = \sigma(W_i \cdot [h_{t-1}, x_t] + b_i) \quad \text{(Input Gate — what new info to add)}$$ $$\tilde{C}_t = \tanh(W_C \cdot [h_{t-1}, x_t] + b_C) \quad \text{(Candidate — new content)}$$ $$C_t = f_t \times C_{t-1} + i_t \times \tilde{C}_t \quad \text{(Cell Update — highway update)}$$ $$o_t = \sigma(W_o \cdot [h_{t-1}, x_t] + b_o) \quad \text{(Output Gate)}$$ $$h_t = o_t \times \tanh(C_t) \quad \text{(New hidden state)}$$

4 · The Attention Mechanism

Everyday picture — the spotlight
You are in a dark room (the encoder's memory). You have a flashlight (attention). When translating "noir", you shine it on the French word "noir" and get 73% brightness. You also shine weakly on "chat" (10%). Your translation uses a weighted mix of all encoder states — not just the last one.

Three steps

Step 1 — Alignment scores (dot product): \(e_j = s_t \cdot h_j\) — how much the decoder state \(s_t\) aligns with encoder state \(h_j\).

Step 2 — Attention weights (softmax): \(\alpha_j = \dfrac{e^{e_j}}{\sum_k e^{e_k}}\) — turn scores into probabilities that sum to 1.

Step 3 — Context vector (weighted sum): \(c_t = \sum_j \alpha_j h_j\) — blend all encoder states by relevance.

Worked example

Decoder state \(s_t = [1,0]^T\). Encoder states: \(h_1 = [1,1]^T\) ("Bank"), \(h_2 = [0,1]^T\) ("River").

$$e_1 = s_t \cdot h_1 = 1\times1 + 0\times1 = 1 \qquad e_2 = s_t \cdot h_2 = 1\times0 + 0\times1 = 0$$ $$\alpha_1 = \frac{e^1}{e^1+e^0} = \frac{2.718}{3.718} \approx 0.73 \qquad \alpha_2 = \frac{e^0}{e^1+e^0} \approx 0.27$$ $$c_t = 0.73 \begin{bmatrix}1\\1\end{bmatrix} + 0.27\begin{bmatrix}0\\1\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}0.73\\1.00\end{bmatrix}$$

The model pays 73% attention to "Bank" and 27% to "River".

5 · The Transformer

Key insight — "Attention is All You Need" (2017)
Throw away the RNN. Instead of processing words one at a time (sequential), process the whole sentence at once using only attention. This enables massive parallelism, which is why Transformers can be trained on internet-scale data.

Self-Attention: the Library analogy

In encoder-decoder attention, the decoder looked at the encoder. In Self-Attention, the sentence looks at itself. Every word asks: "How does every other word in this sentence relate to me?"

Three vectors are created for every word:

VectorSymbolLibrary analogyRole
Query\(Q\)Your search termWhat this word is looking for
Key\(K\)Title on book spineWhat this word advertises about itself
Value\(V\)Book contentWhat this word actually contributes
🧠 Must memorise — Scaled Dot-Product Attention
$$\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) V$$

\(QK^T\): similarity between every query and every key  ·  \(\sqrt{d_k}\): scaling to prevent huge dot products  ·  softmax: convert to probabilities  ·  \(\times V\): weighted mix of values.

Multi-Head Attention: Committee of Advisors

One set of Q/K/V captures one perspective. Running attention h times in parallel captures different aspects simultaneously:

$$\text{MultiHead}(Q,K,V) = \text{Concat}(\text{head}_1, \ldots, \text{head}_h)\,W^O$$

Advisor 1 links "it" → "animal" (subject). Advisor 2 links "it" → "tired" (adjective). Their advice is combined.

The full Transformer block

#LayerWhat it does
1Multi-Head Self-AttentionWords look at each other
2Add & NormResidual \(x + f(x)\) + LayerNorm — prevents vanishing gradients
3Feed-Forward NetworkPosition-wise MLP — the "thinking" step after looking up
4Add & NormResidual again

Positional Encodings

Attention sees all words at once — it has no sense of order. We add position signals:

🧠 Positional Encoding — clock hands analogy
$$PE_{(pos,\,2i)} = \sin\!\left(\frac{pos}{10000^{2i/d_{\text{model}}}}\right) \qquad PE_{(pos,\,2i+1)} = \cos\!\left(\frac{pos}{10000^{2i/d_{\text{model}}}}\right)$$

Different dimensions rotate at different speeds (like second hand vs hour hand). Every position gets a unique "fingerprint".

6 · BERT — Contextual Embeddings

The core problem with static embeddings (Word2Vec)
"bank" always gets vector [0.2, 0.8, …] regardless of context. But "I sat on the river bank" vs "I went to the bank to deposit money" — completely different meanings. Word2Vec cannot tell them apart.

BERT's solution: contextual embeddings

BERT is a stack of Transformer encoders. When it processes "bank" in the river sentence, its self-attention pulls from "river" and "sat". In the money sentence, it pulls from "deposit" and "money". The same word gets a different vector depending on context.

🧠 BERT training objectives

1 · Masked Language Model (MLM): 15% of tokens are masked. BERT must predict them from context — both left and right simultaneously.

Input: "The [MASK] sat on the [MASK]."    Target: "cat", "mat"

$$P(w_i \mid w_1, \ldots, w_{i-1}, w_{i+1}, \ldots, w_n)$$

2 · Next Sentence Prediction (NSP): Given two sentences A and B, predict whether B logically follows A. Teaches long-range sentence relationships.

Fine-tuning: the Swiss Army Knife

Before BERT: one model per task (one for translation, one for QA, one for sentiment). After BERT:

Pre-training vs Fine-tuning
Pre-train on Wikipedia + BooksCorpus (expensive, once). Then fine-tune on your specific task by adding a tiny output layer and training for a few epochs (cheap, minutes). One Swiss Army Knife, many blades.

🧠 The story in one paragraph
RNNs could handle sequences but forgot early words. LSTMs added gates to remember longer. Attention let the decoder look at all encoder states at once. Transformers threw away the RNN entirely and ran attention on everything in parallel. BERT then pre-trained a deep bidirectional Transformer on massive text so that any downstream task only needs a small fine-tuning step — producing embeddings that change meaning with context.
