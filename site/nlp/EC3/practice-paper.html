Answers — RAG & Dense Retrieval

Shared normalisation factor (same for all terms since |D|=7, avgdl=8, b=0.75, k₁=1.5):
$$\text{norm} = 1 - b + b \cdot \frac{|D|}{\text{avgdl}} = 1 - 0.75 + 0.75 \times \frac{7}{8} = 0.25 + 0.65625 = 0.90625$$ $$\text{TF factor} = \frac{tf \cdot (k_1 + 1)}{tf + k_1 \cdot \text{norm}} = \frac{1 \times 2.5}{1 + 1.5 \times 0.90625} = \frac{2.5}{1 + 1.359375} = \frac{2.5}{2.359375} = 1.0596$$
Per-term BM25:
"transformers": 1.2 × 1.0596 = 1.272
"processing": 0.9 × 1.0596 = 0.954
"sequence": 1.0 × 1.0596 = 1.060
"how", "do": tf = 0 → BM25 = 0
BM25(D2, Q) = 1.272 + 0.954 + 1.060 = 3.285
What closed-book cannot do: A closed-book LLM generates answers purely from its parametric weights — it cannot access information beyond its training cutoff, cannot cite sources, and has no mechanism to correct factual errors at inference time. It hallucinates confidently when its training data is insufficient.
RAG Stage 1 — Retrieval: Given a query, a retriever (BM25 or dense bi-encoder) searches a document corpus and returns the top-k most relevant passages.
RAG Stage 2 — Generation: The retrieved passages are concatenated into the prompt as context. The LLM generates its answer conditioned on both the query and the retrieved evidence.
RAG failure mode: retriever failure — if the relevant document is not retrieved (low recall), the LLM still halluccinates because it has no grounding context. Closed-book models do not have this failure mode (they simply answer from memory, for better or worse).
